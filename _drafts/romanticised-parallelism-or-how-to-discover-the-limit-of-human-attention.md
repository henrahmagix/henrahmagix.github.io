I’ve always romanticised organisation and productivity - I guess that’s what happens when you grow up not knowing you have ADHD. Because of that, the lure of “many agents all working in parallel on different things \*for you\*” is intensely appealing!

I do NOT like the term “agents”, and I do NOT like LLMs nor their “harnesses”. And when I tried Claude from pressure at work, I found my whole process became difficult and lacking confidence because \*I\* wasn’t making the changes.

And yet, it’s still appealing… but with all that “work” that gets done, how do you release it when you work in a team? It’s a lot of effort to push onto your colleagues, to review a slate of changes that you most likely haven’t given your full attention. I mean, you \*are\* giving each change your full attention, right?

If you \*are\* giving the same level of attention to every change you push as you would have before LLMs, then you aren’t saving any time, cos there’s only one of you. And you’re only attempting this kind of productivity boost to be more productive, right? What other reasons are there?

Therefore we can safely and confidently presume that you \*are\* paying less attention to your work. That much must be true. If you’re not paying as much attention, then your colleagues must be when they review it. And if they’re not, then who is?

I guess you can just release it and find out later if it was a good change or not. That’s one way of doing things. But when you’re questioned about a change, are you able to recollect and give a confidence answer to how the system is operating and what the consequences are of certain user input and actions? Probably not, right? Cos you weren’t paying as much attention as you used to.

What are we being paid for? I can answer that for myself, confidently.
